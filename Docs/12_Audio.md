# ONE BRAHAM — audio

Audio is the second-most important system in this game after the gunplay, because
of what the game actually is: a building full of people who cannot see each
other. Everything a player knows about a fight two floors up arrives as sound.
That is the design brief, and this document is how it is implemented.

Generated by `Tools/Python/onebraham/build_audio.py`. Replacement recordings go
in at the paths the bank names.

---

## 1. The mix

Twelve sound classes under one master. Three submixes carry the game's two
dynamic behaviours (voice ducking, explosive muffle) and its single most
important one (a gunshot must be heard through a floor).

```
SC_OneBraham_Master                     -6.0 dBFS headroom, limiter
 ├── SC_Master
 │    ├── SC_SFX
 │    │    ├── SC_Weapons               -4.0 dB,  sidechain: none
 │    │    ├── SC_Impact                -6.0 dB
 │    │    └── SC_Footsteps            -12.0 dB
 │    ├── SC_Gameplay                   -3.0 dB  (utility, doors, terminals)
 │    ├── SC_UI                         -9.0 dB  2D only, never attenuated
 │    ├── SC_Music                     -11.0 dB  ducked by the voice bus
 │    ├── SC_Ambience                  -14.0 dB
 │    └── SC_Voice
 │         ├── SC_Dialogue              -2.0 dB
 │         └── SC_Announcements         -1.0 dB  (tannoy: never ducked by anyone)
 └── (buses) SC_Voice, SC_Weapons
```

### The four mixes

| Mix | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SM_OneBraham` | default | the tree above, plus the occlusion and focus filters |
| `SM_OneBraham_VoiceDuck` | a player keys up | 0.45× on ambience, music and footsteps for the duration of the transmission, released over 250 ms |
| `SM_OneBraham_FlashMuffle` | within 1.5 s of a flashbang in line of sight | master low-pass 400 Hz, pitch −8%, tinnitus bed on top |
| `SM_OneBraham_DownedMuffle` | downed | master low-pass 900 Hz, everything else −12 dB, heartbeat and breathing on their own bus |

The duck is ref-counted: it applies while *anyone* is transmitting, so a squad
shouting over each other does not stack into silence.

## 2. Attenuation

Thirteen objects, authored by `Tools/Python/onebraham/build_audio.py` as
`/Game/OneBraham/Audio/Attenuation/SA_OneBraham_*`. All log falloff, all with
occlusion enabled at **900 Hz** and a low-pass on occlusion.

The C++ (`UOneBrahamAudioManagerComponent::GetAttenuation`) resolves the authored
asset for a sound's category and falls back to building an equivalent object at
runtime, so an empty checkout is never silent and a designer's tweak in the
content browser always wins over anything computed in code.

| Object | Category | Inner | Falloff | Spatialise | Focus | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `SA_OneBraham_Interface` | Interface | 250 cm | 250 cm | no | no | UI, hit markers, radio. 2D by construction |
| `SA_OneBraham_Foley` | Foley | 40 cm | 1800 cm | yes | high | footsteps, cloth, gear, magazine handling |
| `SA_OneBraham_Mechanical` | Mechanical | 500 cm | 4500 cm | yes | yes | doors, lifts, reloads, breach plant |
| `SA_OneBraham_WeaponClose` | Weapon | 150 cm | 9000 cm | yes | yes | your own weapon, and nearby ones |
| `SA_OneBraham_WeaponInterior` | Weapon | 150 cm | 14000 cm | yes | yes | through floors: a rifle two floors up |
| `SA_OneBraham_Suppressed` | Weapon | 80 cm | 2600 cm | yes | yes | suppressed pistol; the information weapon |
| `SA_OneBraham_WeaponDistant` | Weapon | 2500 cm | 45000 cm | yes | no | the distant layer of every gunshot and explosion |
| `SA_OneBraham_Impact` | Impact | 60 cm | 6000 cm | yes | yes | bullet impacts, glass, debris |
| `SA_OneBraham_Objective` | Objective | 1500 cm | 12000 cm | yes | no | alarms, extraction, the lockdown |
| `SA_OneBraham_Utility` | Objective | 120 cm | 12000 cm | yes | no | grenades, charges, utility bounces |
| `SA_OneBraham_Announcement` | Objective | 0 cm | 100000 cm | no | no | tannoy, non-spatialised, never ducked |
| `SA_OneBraham_Voice` | Loop | 60 cm | 2400 cm | yes | yes | proximity voice chat |
| `SA_OneBraham_Loop` | Loop | 1600 cm | 30000 cm | yes | no | ambience beds, rotor, shutter loops |

The three weapon rows are the whole point of the table. A rifle fired one floor
above you arrives through `SA_OneBraham_WeaponInterior`: quieter, longer-tailed,
and *unlocalised* — you know which floor, you do not know which room. That
uncertainty is the game.

**When each one is chosen** (`UOneBrahamAudioManagerComponent::PlayWeaponShot`,
which asks `IsMuzzleAboveOrBelowListener` — anything more than
`OneBrahamConstants::HalfFloorHeightCm` = 200 cm away vertically):

| Situation | Object |
| --- | --- |
| Suppressed weapon | `SA_OneBraham_Suppressed` |
| Fired from another storey | `SA_OneBraham_WeaponInterior`, low-passed to 3200 Hz ÷ storeys between |
| Anything else | `SA_OneBraham_WeaponClose` |
| The distant layer, any storey | `SA_OneBraham_WeaponDistant` |

Half a storey is the threshold because 200 cm is exactly the distance at which a
listener is nearer the floor above than the one they are standing on. One number,
used by the audio, the bot hearing model and the map, defined once in
`OneBraham.h`.

## 3. Reverb per area

Real spaces, one profile per layout area. The numbers are `REVERB_PROFILES` in
`build_audio.py`, and they are used twice: as the target for an authored reverb
in a volume per area, and - today, with no reverb assets - as the room tail
every gunshot and explosion excites. The synthesiser renders one
`SFX_Gunshot_Tail_<Profile>` per row at that decay (early reflections from the
nearest walls, a diffuse tail whose top end dies first, and the room's own
character), and the audio manager plays it 2D at the wet level for the area the
**listener** is standing in.

| Area | Tail cue | Decay | Wet | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground Plaza | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Plaza` | 1.8 s | 0.25 | outside: slap-back off the buildings opposite, 150–550 ms |
| Lobby | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Lobby` | 2.6 s | 0.55 | marble and glass, bright early reflections |
| Mezzanine | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Mezzanine` | 1.8 s | 0.28 | carpet and soft furnishing |
| Open Plan (3–8) | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_OpenPlan` | 0.9 s | 0.18 | ceiling tiles, carpet: dark and short. Also the fallback room |
| Server & Comms (9–11) | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_ServerComms` | 1.4 s | 0.30 | metal: a faint ring under the tail |
| Executive (12–14) | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Executive` | 1.1 s | 0.22 | carpet, glass, stillness |
| Canteen (15) | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Canteen` | 2.2 s | 0.45 | hard floor, wide room |
| Stairwell | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Stairwell` | 3.4 s | 0.65 | concrete, with a 19 ms flutter echo between the walls. The loudest place in the game and it should be |
| Skydeck | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Skydeck` | 0.4 s | 0.05 | wind. Almost nothing |

A shot on another storey gets no tail of its own: the interior attenuation is
already the room above heard through concrete.

Crossfade between volumes is 0.6 s, driven by the audio director from the
player's current room, so walking out of the lobby into the stairwell is a
continuous change rather than a switch.

## 4. Ambience beds (11)

One bed per place the audio director can be standing in. The ids, the briefs and
the mix levels live in `Tools/Python/onebraham/build_audio.py` (`AMBIENCE`) and in
the bank's core table; the zone each one plays in is the building layout's own
area id, because a room is described in one place in this project.

| Id | Zone (layout area id) | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `AMB_Plaza_Street` | `Ground_Plaza` | Aldgate traffic, buses, rain on the canopy |
| `AMB_Lobby` | `Lobby` | marble hall: footstep reverb tail, reception hum |
| `AMB_Lift_Lobby` | `Mezzanine` | lift motors, chimes, the draught of a shaft |
| `AMB_Office_Hum` | `OpenPlan` | HVAC, monitors, the odd chair |
| `AMB_Server_Room` | `ServerComms` | a fan wall, cold-aisle rush, alarm chirps. Very loud, and a deliberate in-game disadvantage |
| `AMB_Executive` | `Executive` | near-silence, distant lifts, a clock |
| `AMB_Canteen` | `Canteen` | extractor hoods, fridges, distant clatter |
| `AMB_Skydeck_Wind` | `Skydeck` | wind past the parapet, the city far below |
| `AMB_Stairwell_Door` | `Stairwell` (and any floor with no area) | a concrete tube, fire doors, your own breathing |
| `AMB_Plaza_Rain` | — | rain on glass, drains, a wet forecourt. Layered over the street bed when it rains |
| `AMB_London_Bed` | any unknown zone | the generic city bed. A zone nobody mapped plays this rather than nothing |

All eleven are **synthesised** today: `OneBrahamProceduralAudio.cpp` holds a
recipe per bed (noise colour, two plant tones, one repeating event, a gust) and
bakes a seamless loop, so no room in the building is ever silent, with content or
without. A recorded wave at
`/Game/OneBraham/Audio/Ambience/<Id>` replaces the synthesis the moment it
exists.

The fire alarm is not a bed of its own. It is `SFX_FireAlarm`, held on the
lockdown bed by the manager for as long as the stage lasts, at a level that makes
speech uncomfortable — which is the point of an alarm.

### The zone table, and why it is checked

`AmbienceCueByZone` on `DA_AudioBank` maps the layout's area ids to these beds.
It has been the weak point of this system twice: it was once keyed
`Zone_Plaza`/`Zone_Office`/`Zone_ServerRoom`, a third vocabulary nothing else in
the project used, and the audio manager carried a fourth (`Plaza`, `Offices`,
`ServerRoom`) in a setter that nothing called. `check_audio_ids.py` now compares
the table, the layout asset and the manager: one area id per bed, no orphans in
either direction.

## 5. Music (8 beds)

Dry, patient, mostly electronic, and never a melody you could hum while shooting.
The reference is a corporate building's own ambience being stretched into
something wrong.

| Id | When | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MUS_Lobby` | main menu and deploy screen | slow, warm, deliberately at odds with the match |
| `MUS_MatchEarly` | 0–4 minutes | pulse, no percussion, low mix |
| `MUS_MatchMid` | 4–9 minutes, or any firefight | percussion enters. Still not a "combat loop" |
| `MUS_MatchLate` | ≤4 players alive, or ≥6 kills in a minute | the melody finally arrives, quietly |
| `MUS_Lockdown` | stage `Lockdown` onward | alarm-adjacent, mostly low strings |
| `MUS_Extraction` | `Broadcast` through `Holding` | rotor rhythm as the time signature |
| `MUS_Downed` | while downed | heartbeat, single note, no development |
| `MUS_Results` | end of match | the lobby theme, two semitones lower |

Transitions are driven by `SetMusicState()` in the audio manager, never by hand:
it ignores a request for the state already playing, and it will not cut from
`MUS_Extraction` to anything else except `MUS_Results`.

## 6. Weapon audio: the information design

Every weapon definition carries `AudibleRadius` and `InteriorAudibleRadius`, and
they are the balance levers for the whole game's stealth layer:

| Weapon | Close radius | Interior radius | Suppressed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P99 sidearm | 7000 | 11000 | no |
| LCP machine pistol | 6800 | 10500 | no |
| P99-S suppressed | 2600 | 3200 | **yes** |
| MP5 SMG | 8600 | 13500 | no |
| MP7 PDW | 8800 | 13000 | no |
| M870 pump | 10500 | 16000 | no |
| Semi-auto 12 | 10000 | 15500 | no |
| M4 carbine | 9200 | 14000 | no |
| MCX compact | 9000 | 13800 | no |
| L129A1 marksman | 11000 | 17500 | no |

Two consequences, both deliberate:

* The suppressed pistol has the worst time-to-kill in the game (26 damage, 380
  rpm) and by far the best information profile. It is the weapon you take when
  you intend to be somewhere you should not be.
* The marksman rifle can be heard from the Skydeck stairwell. Firing one is
  announcing your position to the whole building, which is a fair price for the
  ability to end a fight from the far end of the open-plan floor.

### How a gunshot is built

`UOneBrahamAudioManagerComponent::PlayWeaponShot` plays up to five layers for the
local listener. Every layer waits `distance / 343 m/s`, so a shot across a floor
plate is heard after its flash, and a supersonic round's crack
(`PlayNearMissWhizz`) arrives *before* the report - the gap a player judges range by.

| Layer | Cue | When | Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Close report | `SFX_Gunshot_<Calibre>` | always | full to 15 m, falling to 0.2 by 60 m |
| Action | `SFX_Gunshot_Mech` | within 12 m; 2D for your own weapon | fades 4–12 m |
| Distant report | `SFX_Gunshot_Distant_Light` (pistol, SMG) / `_Heavy` (rifle, shotgun, marksman) | past 10 m, dominant past 40 m; also through floors | rises 10–40 m; ≥ 0.6 ÷ storeys through a slab |
| Room tail | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_<Profile>` | same storey | wet × 1.5, ×0.25 suppressed, fades gently with range |
| Shell drop | `SFX_Shell_Drop` / `_Carpet` / `_Metal` | within 14 m, 0.4–0.6 s later, floor traced under the ejection port | carpet swallows it, a steel deck gives it away |

A **suppressed** shot is a quiet cough with the bolt's clack baked in, on the
suppressed curve, with no distant layer and a quarter of the room tail. A shot
from **another storey** keeps `SA_OneBraham_WeaponInterior` and is low-passed
per slab between.

Every synthesised combat cue is rendered as 3–5 variants with different seeds
(grain positions, mode frequencies, decays) and never plays the same variant
twice running; the manager adds ±3–6% pitch on top.

### How an explosion is built

`PlayExplosion(Location, RadiusCm, SoundId)` - and `PlaySoundAtLocation` for the
three blast ids, so breach charges get it too:

| Layer | Cue | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blast | `SFX_Frag_Explosion` / `SFX_Breach_Explosion` / `SFX_Flashbang_Bang` | crack (N-wave), blast body, falling boom, a 28–36 Hz sub tail of 2.6–3 s |
| Debris | `SFX_Explosion_Debris` | 80–160 ms after the blast: chips, glass, a falling fitting. Fades out by 50 m |
| Distant | `SFX_Explosion_Distant` | past 20 m and through floors: rumble and city echoes on the distant curve |
| Room | `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_<Profile>` | wet × 2, pitched down |
| Ears | `SFX_Flashbang_Ring` bed + world low-pass | inside 1.35× radius: 0.9–4.5 s of ringing, world low-passed 1800→450 Hz by strength. Out to 3.5× radius: a 0.3–0.8 s duck to 5000→2200 Hz. A wall between halves it, a floor slab cuts it to a third |

Each weapon also carries `FireSoundClose`, `FireSoundDistant`, `SuppressedFireSound`,
`DryFireSound`, `ReloadSound`, `DrawSound` and `ShellDropSound`, and the audio
manager picks close or distant from the listener's distance and whether there is
architecture between them. Firing a rifle into a stairwell should sound like
firing a rifle into a stairwell; the two-sample approach is what does that
without fifty wave assets.

## 7. Footsteps

Fifteen surfaces, one cue each, scheduled by the audio manager from the physical
material of the surface the character is standing on. There is no per-character
step animation event driving audio — the stride length is calculated from speed
in `TickFootstepAudio`, so a slow walk produces quiet, widely-spaced steps and a
sprint produces loud, close ones, with no asset duplication.

| Surface set | Cue | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marble, Lino | `SFX_Footstep_Hard` | bright, long tail in the lobby |
| Carpet, Fabric | `SFX_Footstep_Carpet` | absorbed, short |
| Concrete, Plasterboard | `SFX_Footstep_Hard` | pitch −6% |
| Metal, Grating, Decking, ServerRack | `SFX_Footstep_Metal` | the giveaway surface |
| Glass, BallisticGlass | `SFX_Footstep_Hard` | creak and crunch. The break itself is `SFX_Window_Break`, played as a one-shot, not as a footstep |
| Wood | `SFX_Footstep_Hard` | pitch −10% |
| Puddle | `SFX_Footstep_Water` | splash, plus a wet footprint decal |

Crouch-walking on carpet is inaudible past six metres. Sprinting on metal is
audible through a floor. Those two facts are how the stealth layer works, and
they come out of the table rather than out of a designer's memory.

## 8. The dynamic layer

| Behaviour | Where | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ducking | `SM_OneBraham_VoiceDuck` | 0.45× beds while anyone transmits, 250 ms release |
| Flashbang | `SetTinnitusState` | 400 Hz low-pass + ringing bed, 1.4–6 s by distance and line of sight |
| Downed | `SetDownedAudioState` | 900 Hz low-pass, world −12 dB, the live heartbeat held at 0.85 (≈140 bpm). A recorded `SFX_Downed_Heartbeat` in the bank replaces it |
| Deafened (spectating) | `SetDeafened` | everything distant, music leads, no heartbeat |
| Low health | `SetHeartbeatIntensity`, and the manager's own 10 Hz poll of the pawn's health | a live heartbeat generator: 64 bpm and silent at 0, 152 bpm and loud at 1; driven from 40% health down to 10%, the loudest request wins. Never muffled |
| Blast | `PlayExplosion` | ringing and muffle by distance and exposure, see section 6 |
| Purge | `SetLockdownAudio` | alarm bed + sprinkler, and the purge tick tone every 0.2 s |
| Extraction | `SetExtractionAudio` | rotor bed, 2D sub-layer, positioned on the pad, speeding up from `Broadcast` to `Arriving` |

`SetExtractionAudio` is driven by `UOneBrahamExtractionComponent::ApplyLocalExtractionAudio` on every machine that has a local player, from the replicated state and the arrival alpha the floodlight already uses (`Docs/09` section 2.8): called at 10 Hz, pushed only when the state changes or the approach moves by half a percent. The winch being called is the loudest cue in the mode and it is the one that tells the rest of the building where the squad is, which is the whole point of the ten-second broadcast that precedes the flight.
| Occlusion | every attenuation object | 900 Hz low-pass, 0.15 s trace interval per listener |
| Concurrency | the manager | per-cue limits: 4 gunshots, 6 impacts, 2 explosions, 8 footsteps |

## 9. Cue ids and the recording brief

`DA_AudioBank` names every cue the code plays. Three categories exist in the
build today:

1. **Present as assets** — recorded or licensed, referenced from the bank.
2. **Synthesised fallback** — the audio manager generates the wave at runtime
   (per-calibre gunshot bodies, impacts, UI blips). A build with zero audio
   content still plays a match that sounds like a match.
3. **Recording briefs** — cues that are neither recorded nor synthesised and are
   listed in the build report with exactly what to record.

### Entry points for gameplay code

Every gameplay system reaches the local mix through
`UOneBrahamAudioManagerComponent::GetLocal(WorldContext)`, which is null on a
dedicated server. The cue wrappers:

| Call | Owner of the event |
| --- | --- |
| `PlayWeaponShot(Definition, Muzzle, bSuppressed, Shooter)` | the weapon, on every machine |
| `PlayImpactSound(ImpactInfo)` / `PlayNearMissWhizz(Location, bSupersonic)` | `UOneBrahamVFXStatics` |
| `PlayExplosion(Location, RadiusCm, SoundId)` | the grenade's detonation multicast |
| `PlayDamageTakenCue(bArmourAbsorbed, bHeadshot)` | the controller's damage notification |
| `PlayBodyFall(Location, ImpactSpeed, Surface)` | death, the ragdoll's corpse impact, a downed collapse |
| `PlayShellDrop(Location, Surface, Pitch)` | called by `PlayWeaponShot` itself; public for pump actions |
| `SetHeartbeatIntensity(0..1)` | the character, if it wants more than the health-driven default |

The briefs are the shopping list. Each one specifies duration, character,
frequency target, and what it must *communicate* — because "record a shutter
sound" produces a shutter sound nobody can locate in a building.


### Synthesised today

Everything below plays on a clean checkout, rendered by
`OneBrahamProceduralAudio.cpp`; a recording at the bank's path replaces it the
moment it exists, and the brief is what that recording must do.

| Cue | What the synthesis is | Brief for the recording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SFX_Gunshot_Pistol` `SFX_Gunshot_SMG` `SFX_Gunshot_Rifle` `SFX_Gunshot_Shotgun` `SFX_Gunshot_Marksman` | N-wave crack, two-stage blast, falling thump, a cavity pop centred lower per calibre | close-mic'd report, 250 ms tail, no room |
| `SFX_Gunshot_Suppressed` | baffled cough, gas "pfft", bolt back and forward | mechanical action dominant, unlocatable past 30 m |
| `SFX_Gunshot_Mech` | bolt carrier back and into battery: two metal strikes 30–50 ms apart | the action alone, close-mic'd |
| `SFX_Gunshot_Distant_Light` `SFX_Gunshot_Distant_Heavy` | dark boom, 4–7 echoes off the city, rolling rumble | a shot from 150 m across buildings |
| `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Lobby` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Mezzanine` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_OpenPlan` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_ServerComms` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Executive` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Canteen` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Skydeck` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Plaza` `SFX_Gunshot_Tail_Stairwell` | early reflections + darkening diffuse tail per room (section 3) | an impulse response of the real room, direct sound removed |

| `UI_Damage_Taken` | a low thud in the head | never confusable with the hit marker |
| `UI_Banner` | the Tannoy's two-tone chime | a soft two-note before a line of announcement |
| `UI_Voice_Radio_Open` `UI_Voice_Radio_Close` | click and band-limited "tsk"; click and squelch tail | sits under the first syllable, never on top |
| `UI_Voice_ChannelSwitch` | one detent, pitched by the caller (squad up, proximity down) | readable without looking |
| `UI_Voice_Connected` `UI_Voice_Disconnected` `UI_Voice_Muted` | rising pair, falling pair, a dead click | radio on, off, and somebody has stopped listening |

## 10. Voice chat

The engine `Voice` module, 16 kHz opus, 20 ms frames over unreliable RPC into a
per-speaker `USoundWaveProcedural`. Details are in
`Docs/07_Multiplayer_Voice.md`; the audio-side consequences are:

* Proximity channel: `SA_OneBraham_Voice`, 2400 cm falloff, occluded by the same
  900 Hz low-pass as everything else. Standing behind a glass wall reduces a
  voice the way it reduces a footstep.
* Squad channel: radio-processed, 3200 Hz band-pass, 2D, no attenuation.
* Team channel (FIRE DRILL): radio, as above.
* Self-monitoring is off; the game plays a radio click instead. Hearing your own
  voice 100 ms late is what makes players stop using voice chat.
