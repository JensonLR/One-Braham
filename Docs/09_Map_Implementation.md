# ONE BRAHAM — map implementation

The building: 15 floors, 7 areas, 40 rooms, one vertical line from the ground
plaza to the Skydeck. This document is how it is built, why each area is shaped
the way it is, and what every space is *for* as a fight.

The geometry is generated by `Tools/Python/onebraham/build_environment.py` at
true dimensions, so everything below is a description of something that exists in
the map rather than a plan for something that might.

---

## 1. The vertical structure

```
 Floor  Z (cm)   Area                          What it is for
 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  15    5600     Canteen + Skydeck balcony     the ending: canteen cover, then the 30 s hold
  12-14 4400-5200 Executive                    the quiet floors; boardroom long push
  9-11  3200-4000 Server & Comms               the loud floor; cold-aisle sightlines
  3-8   800-2800 Open Plan Office              the main battleground
  2     400      Mezzanine / Lift Lobby        the decision floor
  1     0        Lobby                         first contact, best-looking room
  0     -400     Ground Plaza / Entrance       the last normal minute
```

Floor to floor is 400 cm. That is a real floor height, and it is what makes the
stairwells the vertical spine of the game: from any stairwell landing you can
hear a firefight two floors up and hear a fire door close one floor down.

### Routes up

| Route | Cost | Cost in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lift core | one keycard, fast, loud | Stops at floor 3 and 9 only. Whoever holds the lobby holds the lift |
| Stairwell A (west) | free, slow, audible | No cover, no doors, four storeys of concrete that carry sound |
| Stairwell B (east) | free, slow, visible from the atrium | The fast route if nobody is watching the mezzanine |
| Breach | a charge, 3 s, whole floor hears it | Cuts a hole in four specific partitions; not a general route |
| Terminal override | 12 s alone, 3 s paired, alarm | The co-op route, and the only way past a card nobody holds |

Every route is a decision with a cost. Nothing is free, and nothing is a
shortcut a player can take without somebody knowing.

## 2. The floor table, and why it is an asset

Every floor in this document has a number, an area and a name. The level is built
from one table of those (`common.AREAS` in the content pipeline) and the game
describes the level from another (`UOneBrahamBuildingLayoutAsset`). That is two
tables, two languages, one building, and the failure mode when they drift is
specific and silent: the floor plan tells a player they are on the executive floor
while they are standing in the server hall, the lockdown seals the wrong room, and
the audio director scores a boardroom with a data-centre hum.

So the numbers live in one place, and `check_building_layout.py` compares the
game's table against the map's table floor by floor and fails the build if they
disagree. The table:

| Floor | Area id | The lift says | The plan says | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `Ground_Plaza` | Ground plaza and reception | PLAZA | spawn, turnstiles |
| 1 | `Lobby` | Lobby | LOBBY | reception island, lift bank |
| 2 | `Mezzanine` | Mezzanine and lift lobby | MEZZANINE | atrium bridge |
| 3–8 | `OpenPlan` | Open plan office | OFFICES | breachable on 5 |
| 9–11 | `ServerComms` | Server and comms | SERVER / COMMS | breachable on 9 |
| 12 | `Executive` | Executive wing and boardroom | EXECUTIVE | breachable on 12 |
| 13 | `Executive` | Executive offices | EXECUTIVE | |
| 14 | `Executive` | Executive suites | EXECUTIVE | breachable on 14 |
| 15 | `Skydeck` | Canteen and Skydeck balcony | CANTEEN / SKYDECK | the winch |

Floors 1–14 are one typical floor plate, 4400 × 3600 cm, centred on the lift
core: the same core, stairs, lifts and curtain wall on every floor, column-free
around the core, so the floors differ only in their room programmes. The ground
floor and floor 15 are the exceptions.

`/Game/OneBraham/Data/DA_BuildingLayout` is the authored asset. It is optional by
design: the compiled-in default on the class is the shipping building, so a clean
checkout with no content still describes the tower correctly, and the asset exists
so a designer can move a floor without a programmer. The asset also carries the
breachable-wall flags, which is why the plan's breach warnings cannot point at a
wall the map does not have.

What reads it: the floor plan (`UOneBrahamFloorPlanWidget`), the lift
announcements, the spawn selector, the lockdown director's floor names, the audio
director's zone mapping, and the QA console. Anything that used to invent its own
floor-name table now asks this one.

### Area by area

### 2.1 Ground Plaza and Entrance — floor 0

**Layout.** 5200 × 4400 cm. A canopy, a revolving door, a bank of four
turnstiles, a commissionaire's desk, a bike store and a loading bay set back on
the left.

**Fights here.** Opening rush and the last stand of a failed run. The plaza is
open, so it is decided by who controls the turnstiles — three lanes, no cover
between them, and a desk beside them that is exactly wide enough for two.

**Spawn safety.** Three anchors (one per squad cluster), minimum 2,000 cm
*horizontally on the same storey* between opposing clusters. The loading bay is a
separate spawn pocket so a squad cannot be caught in the open at the doors.

**Streaming.** In the persistent level, always loaded, because it is where every
match starts and a hitch at spawn is the worst possible first impression.

### 2.2 Lobby — floor 1

**Layout.** 4400 × 3600 cm. Reception island (700 × 400 cm) centre, waiting
seating, three lifts in a dark metal bank, four steel columns, two large planters.

**Fights here.** The first real fight. The reception island is the best cover in
the room and the four columns make the room a set of four lanes, so the lobby is
about choosing a lane and holding it.

**Chokepoints.** The lift bank (three doors, one corridor), the atrium bridge to
the mezzanine (one route, visible from above), and the stair door on the west
wall.

**Sightlines.** 44 m diagonal across the marble, broken at 12 m by the planters.
Long enough for a marksman to matter, broken enough that they cannot own it.

### 2.3 Mezzanine and Lift Lobby — floor 2

**Layout.** 4000 × 3200 cm. A bridge across the atrium, glass meeting pods, a
coffee point, a soft seating area.

**Fights here.** The decision floor. Every route up is visible from the bridge
and the bridge is visible from everywhere. Players cross it because it is 40 m
faster than the stairs and they can see that it is a bad idea at the same moment
they decide to do it.

**Why the pods exist.** Glass boxes are the best cover in the game: they stop
bullets, they let you see, and breaking one is a sound cue everyone hears.

### 2.4 Open Plan Office — floors 3–8

**Layout.** 4400 × 3600 cm per floor. Desk farms A and B, two meeting pods, a
break-out kitchen, a print alcove, four offices, two wind-core plant rooms. The
room programme is identical floor to floor with different furniture arrangement,
because that is what an office building is.

**Fights here.** The main battleground of a 25-minute match, and it has to hold
up for eight of them. Desks give crouch cover, screens give sightline breaks,
cabinets give full cover, and the print alcove is the only place with a
one-way-visible angle into the corridor.

**Sightlines.** 38 m down the long axis, cut at 9 m by the acoustic screens.
Scanning is possible; camping is not, because every desk row has a flank.

**Vertical.** The wind-core plant rooms (two per floor) are the only interior
rooms with a floor above and below: they are where a firefight becomes a
grenade fight.

### 2.5 Server and Comms — floors 9–11

**Layout.** 3200 × 2800 cm. Four rack rows with cold-aisle containment, a CRAC
wall, a UPS bank, cable trays overhead, the NOC workstation, a comms cupboard.

**Fights here.** The room is a sightline trap: four rows create long, narrow,
perfectly symmetrical lanes with no cover inside them. Whoever takes the NOC has
the room; whoever walks the lanes does not come back.

**Audio.** It is the loudest room in the building (fan loops at 70 dB
equivalent), so it is the only room where two players can be 5 m apart and
neither hears the other. That is a deliberate tactical affordance and it is
implemented as an audio bed, not as a gameplay flag.

**Access.** Tier `Secure` (IT Operations badge), with the two-panel override on
this floor: the designed co-op moment of the game.

### 2.6 Executive — floor 12

**Layout.** 3600 × 3000 cm. Boardroom (3.2 m table, twelve chairs), corner
office, PA desk, two offices, a bar pavilion, wet room.

**Fights here.** The boardroom is a long, brave push: one table, twelve chairs,
two doors, and nowhere to be. It is the most cinematic room in the game and it
punishes anybody who tries to hold it.

**Access.** Tier `Executive`, with a paired zone between the floor and the east
wing, so the two-panel rule appears twice in a match rather than once.

### 2.7 Executive — floors 12–14

**Layout.** The typical floor plate. The boardroom wing on 12, executive offices
on 13 and suites on 14, each with the boardroom, corner office, PA desk and
lounge programme. Breach walls on 12 and 14.

### 2.8 Canteen and Skydeck — floor 15

**Ceilings.** None, anywhere in the tower: like the real building ("OB"), every
floor shows its bare concrete soffit with steel beams, ducts, pipes and cable trays
hung under it.

**Layout.** 4800 × 4000 cm, split by a glass screen. South of it, under a
roof, the canteen, laid out from the real building's staff canteen: the till
and coffee bar on the back (west) wall; to its right the hot-food line, with the
kitchen behind it (wet floor, one staff door); in front of those, three
timber-clad servery islands; then the dining hall, eighteen six-seat tables
widening out east towards the Skydeck gate. North of the screen, open to the
sky, the Skydeck balcony.

**Fights here.** The canteen is the densest cover in the game: eighteen tables
mean eighteen fire positions, the servery islands and the hot-food counter are
waist-high hard cover, and the kitchen is the one place to break line of sight. The screen is ordinary glass, so the canteen and the balcony can
see each other, and the one door in it - the Skydeck gate - is where the last
push happens. Squads arrive with one down, no utility and less than a magazine
each, and the design intent is that it *feels* like that.

**The balcony.** Extraction pad (900 cm hold radius) marked in safety
yellow, the winch rig, three air-handling units, four floodlight masts, a steel
balustrade, planters, a winch control room.

**Fights here.** The ending, and the only place the game deliberately holds a
player still. The pad is exposed on three sides with cover on one: the AHU boxes.
The hold is 30 seconds. Contested freezes the timer, so the fight is over the pad
itself, not the clock.

**Lighting.** Dusk, floodlit pools, hard shadows. The deck is the most beautiful
frame in the game and it is the one that goes on the store page.

**Extraction.** `Broadcast` 10 s → `Arriving` 45 s → `Holding` (30 s, frozen when
contested) → `Complete`. A failed run ends on this deck, in view of the
helicopter, which is the correct place for it.

**Interactables.** Two actors, and the mode is unplayable without them. The winch
console (`AOneBrahamExtractionTerminal`) is bolted to the pad-facing wall of the
winch control room, facing the deck, and it is the only caller of
`ServerCallExtraction` in the project: hold `E` for 1.4 s
(`AOneBrahamExtractionTerminal::CallHoldSeconds`, the length of
`AM_OB_Interact_Radio`) and the winch is called. Stand with your back to the pad
to use it, which is the decision the whole mode is built around. The winch pad
(`AOneBrahamExtractionPad`) is the 900 cm hold volume
(`C.EXTRACTION_HOLD_RADIUS_CM`, the same number as
`UOneBrahamMatchSettingsAsset::ExtractionZoneRadiusCm`): it registers the wire's
position with the extraction component at BeginPlay, and its floodlight is the
only light on the deck that moves — dark on standby, sweeping while the
helicopter is inbound, hard on the pad while a squad holds it, red while it is
contested. The console's screen mesh wants `M_OB_Screen` from the art pass
(`Docs/10` section 2): its `ScreenTint` parameter is what the console writes, and
with any other material the four states are told through the screen light alone.

## 4. Built-in safety and fairness

Built into the generator, not bolted on afterwards:

| Rule | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum distance between opposing spawn anchors, **same storey** | 2,000 cm | no spawn-kills in the first five seconds |
| Minimum distance between same-cluster anchors | 600 cm | a squad spawns together but not inside each other |
| Vertical distance exempts the enemy rule | 400 cm (one storey) | a slab is a real barrier; the stairwells are the only route between floors |
| Spawn re-validation | cluster distances re-checked at place time, per storey | the generator reports every offending pair by name and floor |
| Every room has two exits | assert in the room programme | no room in this building is a death trap |
| No spawn in a room with a single exit | generator rule | a player must never be spawned into a corner |
| Navmesh rebuild on door and shutter state change | `AddDirtyArea` on the changed bounds | the AI and the geometry cannot disagree |
| 26 spawn anchors, 3 per area (4 in the two biggest) | `ANCHORS` in `build_environment.py` | every area can host every squad, so no squad is ever forced into the same room as another |
| Skydeck is never sealed by the lockdown | `bAllowLockdownClose = false` on that shutter | the building can trap you, it can never trap you away from the only way out |

## 5. What the map is responsible for

The map is not scenery. Three systems read the building itself, and each of them
reads a different property of the geometry the generator places. This section is
the contract between the level and those systems, and two of the three are
enforced by `validate_project.py`.

### 5.1 Floor finishes: fifteen surfaces, not one

Every floor is built as a slab in its area's material, and then every room gets a
finish laid on top of it — 4 mm proud, inset by the partition thickness, labelled
`F<floor>_<Room>_Finish`.

The finish is what the player's footsteps resolve from, what the bullet impact
family resolves from, and what the impact decal is chosen from. Without it the
whole tower is carpet, which sounds wrong, reacts wrong and is the single
cheapest way to make a game built inside one building feel like one room.

| Surface | Where it is, in the building |
| --- | --- |
| Marble | lobby slab, reception island, turnstile banks, lift lobby |
| Carpet | desk farms, meeting rooms, corner offices, waiting seating, breakout |
| Concrete | plaza slab, loading bay, under-stair stores, core, stairwell landings, every bare soffit |
| Metal | lift doors, handrails, riser cupboards, the exposed beams, ducts, pipes and cable trays under every soffit, the coffee bar's back wall |
| Glass / BallisticGlass | the perimeter glazing bands on floors 2–15 |
| Wood | the boardroom, the Skydeck bar, the canteen's servery islands, hot-food counter, till and tables |
| Puddle | kitchen floor, bike store — deliberately wet, deliberately loud |
| Grating | plant rooms, power rooms, comms cupboards, the Skydeck plant deck |
| Fabric | the acoustic meeting pods on the mezzanine and in open plan |
| ServerRack | the raised floor of the cold aisle, both server rows and the NOC |
| Plasterboard | every partition, the kitchen wall, all four breachable walls |
| Lino | canteen hall, servery, hot-food line, coffee bar, break-out kitchen, print alcove, security desk |
| Decking | the Skydeck balcony, the helipad, the edge rail |
| Flesh | gameplay only — a character hit resolves to it; no geometry uses it |

Two of these have gameplay attached, which is why the table matters:
**ServerRack** and **Grating** are the surfaces that throw sparks when shot, so
the server floors have to be rack floor rather than carpet for the sparks to
happen at all, and **Puddle** is the only surface that produces water spray.

The generator records where every surface ended up in the build manifest, and the
validator fails if an authored surface is used by nothing.

### 5.2 Effects the map triggers

The building is what makes the effects happen: a partition gives dust, a rack
gives sparks, a fall gives a body landing. `Docs/11` carries the full
seventeen-effect table with its call sites; the map's half of it is:

| The map provides | The effect that follows |
| --- | --- |
| Four breachable plasterboard partitions | `BreachExplosion`, `BreachDebris`, `DustPuff`, wall collapse audio |
| Rack floor and grating | `SparkShower` on every hit |
| Kitchen and bike-store floors | `WaterSpray` |
| Ballistic glazing on every floor edge | `GlassImpact`, glass decals, shatter audio |
| Fire shutters on floors 0–13 | `DustPuff` on the closing edge, 2.75 s of mechanism audio |
| Stairwell drops of one to three storeys | `CorpseImpact` when the body lands |
| The Skydeck pad in the open | smoke, flashes and the extraction fight happening in view of the helicopter |

### 5.3 Lighting, grade and audio, per floor

Each streaming floor level is created by `build_floor_level`, which places four
things besides the geometry, so a new floor gets all of them without anyone
remembering to add them — which is the point of generating the map rather than
placing it.

**Ceiling light grid.** `RectLight` panels on a 600 cm grid across the whole
floor plate, 4000 K, 2600 cd, 700 cm attenuation, static mobility. The count is
derived from the floor's own footprint, so the plaza gets a grid appropriate to
9000 cm² and a meeting pod does not get one of its own. This is the game's
central visual contrast: fourteen floors of flat, faintly green office light, one
floor of London weather.

**One unbound `PostProcessVolume` per floor**, carrying that area's grade:
manual exposure range, bloom, film grain and vignette, from `AREA_GRADE`. The
ranges are wide on purpose — clamped exposure is what stops a player walking out
of the plant room into the glazing and being flash-blinded for a second and a
half.

| Area | Exposure range | Bloom | Grain | Vignette |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground Plaza | 0.35 – 2.4 | 0.85 | 0.030 | 0.42 |
| Lobby | 0.40 – 2.2 | 0.75 | 0.028 | 0.38 |
| Mezzanine | 0.45 – 2.1 | 0.70 | 0.026 | 0.36 |
| Open Plan | 0.60 – 1.9 | 0.55 | 0.022 | 0.30 |
| Server & Comms | 0.22 – 1.6 | 0.40 | 0.034 | 0.46 |
| Executive | 0.65 – 2.0 | 0.60 | 0.020 | 0.32 |
| Skydeck | 0.55 – 3.6 | 1.15 | 0.024 | 0.44 |

**No reflection captures, deliberately.** The project runs Lumen, which builds
its own reflection scene from the same geometry and lights. A per-floor capture
actor would be a capture that is never sampled and a second source of truth for
reflections — the kind of thing that produces a bug where one room reflects a
layout that was deleted three months ago.

**One `AudioVolume` per floor**, scaled to the plate, enabled at priority 1. The
volume is the map telling the audio director where the room boundaries are; the
ambience bed itself is chosen from the zone id in the audio bank (`AMB_*`), so
the level and the mix cannot disagree about where the server room starts.

**One `NavMeshBoundsVolume` per floor**, sized to the plate. Combined with the
`AddDirtyArea` calls the doors and shutters make when they change state, this is
what stops the AI believing in a wall that was breached ten seconds ago.

## 6. Rebuilding and changing the map

```
Scripts\BuildContent.bat --only environment     rebuild the map
Scripts\BuildContent.bat --only environment,props --dress   and dress it
```

The builder writes `Tools/Python/onebraham_build_manifest.json`: every area,
floor, room, spawn anchor, access zone, terminal, breach wall, shutter and
surface, with locations. That manifest is what `validate_project.py` checks the
level against, which is how a level edit by hand gets caught before it becomes a
mystery bug three weeks later.

Adding a floor means adding an entry to `AREAS` and `ROOM_PROGRAMMES` in
`build_environment.py`. Adding a route up means adding an entry to `ACCESS_ZONES`.
Nothing in the map is hand-placed that can be generated, and everything that is
generated has a name you can search for in the outliner.
