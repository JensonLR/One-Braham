# ONE BRAHAM — map implementation

The building: 18 storeys (ground to 17), 9 areas, 48 rooms, one vertical line from the ground
plaza to the Skydeck. This document is how it is built, why each area is shaped
the way it is, and what every space is *for* as a fight.

The geometry is generated by `Tools/Python/onebraham/build_environment.py` at
true dimensions, so everything below is a description of something that exists in
the map rather than a plan for something that might.

---

## 1. The vertical structure

```
 Floor  Z (cm)   Area                          What it is for
 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  15    5600     Canteen + Skydeck balcony     the ending: canteen cover, then the 30 s hold
  12-14 4400-5200 Executive                    the quiet floors; boardroom long push
  9-11  3200-4000 Server & Comms               the loud floor; cold-aisle sightlines
  3-8   800-2800 Open Plan Office              the main battleground
  2     400      Mezzanine / Lift Lobby        the decision floor
  1     0        Lobby                         first contact, best-looking room
  0     -400     Ground Plaza / Entrance       the last normal minute
```

Floor to floor is 400 cm. That is a real floor height, and it is what makes the
stairwells the vertical spine of the game: from any stairwell landing you can
hear a firefight two floors up and hear a fire door close one floor down.

### Routes up

| Route | Cost | Cost in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lift core | one keycard, fast, loud | Nine cars, every floor, until the Lockdown stage stops them. Whoever holds the lobby holds the lift |
| Stairwell A (west) | free, slow, audible | No cover, no doors, four storeys of concrete that carry sound |
| Stairwell B (east) | free, slow, visible from the atrium | The fast route if nobody is watching the mezzanine |
| Breach | a charge, 3 s, whole floor hears it | Cuts a hole in four specific partitions; not a general route |
| Terminal override | 12 s alone, 3 s paired, alarm | The co-op route, and the only way past a card nobody holds |

Every route is a decision with a cost. Nothing is free, and nothing is a
shortcut a player can take without somebody knowing.

### Lifts

Nine `AOneBrahamLift` actors (`Source/OneBraham/Lifts/`): two banks of four
facing each other across the core hall (west shafts at x = −300 with doors
facing +X, east shafts at x = +300 facing −X, at y = −750, −450, −150 and 150),
and the glass car at (0, 710) with its doors facing −Y onto the back of the hall.
The map builder places each actor at its shaft centre at floor 0's level
(Z = −400), unrotated, and sets its properties; the actor builds its own car,
car doors, landing doors on every served floor, and the call buttons.

* **Moving.** 250 cm/s with 160 cm/s² ramps at each end: one storey in about
  3.2 s, plaza to Skydeck in about 25.6 s. The server replicates the trip (start
  height, start speed, target floor, start time), not a position; every machine
  computes the same curve from it, so a rider never jitters. The car floor is the
  movement base riders stand on.
* **Calling.** The button beside each landing door calls the car, or holds its
  doors if it is already there. Inside, Up and Down move the chosen floor one
  storey per press. Cars serve calls in the direction they are already going.
  Doors take 0.9 s to move and stay open 2.5 s, and reopen for anybody standing
  in the doorway.
* **Lockdown.** At the Lockdown stage every car drops its calls, stops at the
  nearest floor it can brake for, opens its doors and stays open, and refuses
  every button until the stage drops back. A car never finishes a trip to the
  Skydeck in lockdown: the last climb is the stairs. PreLockdown leaves the lifts
  running, because the warning is only useful if people can act on it.

## 2. The floor table, and why it is an asset

Every floor in this document has a number, an area and a name. The level is built
from one table of those (`common.AREAS` in the content pipeline) and the game
describes the level from another (`UOneBrahamBuildingLayoutAsset`). That is two
tables, two languages, one building, and the failure mode when they drift is
specific and silent: the floor plan tells a player they are on the executive floor
while they are standing in the server hall, the lockdown seals the wrong room, and
the audio director scores a boardroom with a data-centre hum.

So the numbers live in one place, and `check_building_layout.py` compares the
game's table against the map's table floor by floor and fails the build if they
disagree. The table:

| Floor | Area id | The lift says | The plan says | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `Ground_Plaza` | Ground plaza and reception | PLAZA | spawn, turnstiles |
| 1 | `Lobby` | Lobby | LOBBY | reception island, lift bank |
| 2 | `Mezzanine` | Mezzanine and lift lobby | MEZZANINE | atrium bridge |
| 3–8 | `OpenPlan` | Open plan office | OFFICES | breachable on 5 |
| 9–11 | `ServerComms` | Server and comms | SERVER / COMMS | breachable on 9 |
| 12 | `Executive` | Executive wing and boardroom | EXECUTIVE | breachable on 12 |
| 13 | `Executive` | Executive offices | EXECUTIVE | |
| 14 | `Executive` | Executive suites | EXECUTIVE | breachable on 14 |
| 15 | `Skydeck` | Canteen and Skydeck balcony | CANTEEN / SKYDECK | the winch |

Floors 1–14 are one typical floor plate, 4400 × 3600 cm, centred on the lift
core: the same core, stairs, lifts and curtain wall on every floor, column-free
around the core, so the floors differ only in their room programmes. The ground
floor and floor 15 are the exceptions.

`/Game/OneBraham/Data/DA_BuildingLayout` is the authored asset. It is optional by
design: the compiled-in default on the class is the shipping building, so a clean
checkout with no content still describes the tower correctly, and the asset exists
so a designer can move a floor without a programmer. The asset also carries the
breachable-wall flags, which is why the plan's breach warnings cannot point at a
wall the map does not have.

What reads it: the floor plan (`UOneBrahamFloorPlanWidget`), the lift
announcements, the spawn selector, the lockdown director's floor names, the audio
director's zone mapping, and the QA console. Anything that used to invent its own
floor-name table now asks this one.

### Area by area

### The core — every floor

Every floor is the same 4400 × 3600 cm plate round the same core, laid out from
the real building's ground floor:

* **The lift hall** runs north–south between two banks of four lifts facing each
  other (shafts at x ±310). Lifts are actors (`AOneBrahamLift`); until a build
  has the class, closed landing doors stand in the openings.
* **The glass lift** stands at the back (north) of the hall.
* **The cross-hallway** runs left and right between the banks and the glass
  lift, out to the floor loop on both sides. Off it: the men's toilets on the
  left, the women's on the right, three cubicles and a vanity each.
* **The main stair**, left of the glass lift, goes from the ground floor to the
  roof; an escape stair mirrors it on the right. Each is a switchback behind a
  stair lobby whose 150 cm door is what the lockdown's fire shutter seals.
* **Every slab is cut** over the stairs and the lift shafts (and, on floor 1,
  over the escalators), so the stairs climb and the cars travel.
* **The loop.** Above the ground floor, each floor loops round the core, with
  rooms in four bands between the loop and the curtain wall. Every room's door
  faces the core.

### 2.1 Ground floor — reception, gates, escalators, coffee shop

Walk in through one of **three revolving doors** in the glass frontage and
**reception** is in front of you: a stone desk in front of a dark feature wall.
To the right is the **line of keycard gates** (speed-gate pedestals every
110 cm). Behind the gates, straight ahead, is the **lift hall**; to its right,
also behind the gates, the **escalators** up to floor 1 in their enclosure. To
the right of the escalators, behind a wall, the **coffee shop** - which opens
onto the public side in front of the gates, not behind them. Back-of-house
behind reception: a security office and a post room; a bike store in the north
corner.

**Fights here.** The opening rush. Everything north of the gates is reached
through the gate lanes, so the first fight of a match is about the gate line:
lanes with nothing between them, a reception desk that is exactly wide enough
for two, and a coffee shop on the flank.

**Which way is right.** Unreal is left-handed: facing north (+Y) from the
doors, your right hand is **-X**. The public side - reception's gate line,
the escalators, the coffee shop - is therefore built at -X, and the core's
stairs at +X are on the left of the glass lift, as in the real building. Each
speed-gate pedestal carries its own **glass wings, held permanently open**:
folded flat against its sides, inside its length, standing above it.

**Reception, as built.** Double height: floor 1's slab is open over the
reception side, with a glass gallery round it. A black linear-baffle ceiling
with sweeping light ribbons; a black feature volume behind the desk carrying an
**OLED screen** running adverts; an open steel **feature stair** with timber
treads along the east glazing, up to the gallery; the glazed base in black
mullions with glass fins.

**The main stair is a spiral.** Through the stair door on the left of the
glass lift (+x, facing it from the lift hall) a spiral runs the full height of
the tower - twenty treads a storey round a steel column, one turn per floor.
The other core stair is the switchback fire stair; the real building has two.

**The gates and the escalators.** Through the revolving doors, reception is
ahead and the main line of card gates to its right; through them, the lift hall
is straight on, and to the right, along the passage at the foot of the
escalators, you turn left up **four escalators** side by side. A short wall ends
the main gate line; beyond it a **second line of card gates** stands right in
front of the escalators, on the public side. Every gate's glass wings are held
open. The escalators rise between sloped glass balustrades with black handrails.

**The OLED displays** (`AOneBrahamDisplay`): a double-height screen wall the
whole length of the escalator run, a screen ceiling over the whole run, one on
the back wall right in front of you at the top - after which the only way on is
left, then left again past the lift banks - and one over the reception desk.
Each runs a loop of fictional adverts in the building's own voice: an eyebrow in
tracked capitals with an accent rule, a headline, a subline, laid out on a
margin grid scaled to the panel, the copy fading out and in at each change while
the aurora field behind it (`M_OB_AdScreen`: soft bands on true black, a
breathing glow, a vignette at the bezel) crossfades to the next advert's
colours. Neighbouring screens are phased so they never change together; the
ceiling runs the field without copy.

### 2.2 Floor 1 — arrival gallery, showcase, lab

The escalators arrive into a stone gallery along the south side, behind a glass
balustrade round the escalator opening. A client showcase on the west, a lab on
the east, a meeting room and a coffee point on the north.

### 2.3 Floor 2 — breakout and meeting rooms

Breakout seating on the west, team desks along the south, two glass meeting
pods on the east, a quiet room and a vending alcove on the north.

### 2.4 Open Plan Office — floors 3–8

Desk farms on the west, east and south: benching in back-to-back pairs with a
low screen, in rows with aisles - crouch cover everywhere, full cover nowhere. A
break-out kitchen and a print alcove in the south corners, two meeting pods on
the north. The programme is identical floor to floor, because that is what an
office building is.

### 2.5 Server and Comms — floors 9–11

Two server halls, west and east, full-height walls, 2 m rack rows with cold
aisles between them: long, narrow, symmetrical lanes. The NOC along the south,
the power room and comms cupboard on the north. The loudest floors in the
building. Tier `Secure`, with the two-panel override on floor 9.

### 2.6 Executive — floors 12–14

The boardroom along the south between two corner offices, an executive suite on
the west, the PA desk and the executive lounge on the east. Tier `Executive`,
with a paired zone between the floor and the east wing. Breach walls on 12 and
14.

### 2.7 Ceilings

None, anywhere in the tower: like the real building ("OB"), every floor shows
its bare concrete soffit with steel beams, ducts, pipes and cable trays hung
under it, stopping at the core so nothing hangs in a stairwell.

### 2.8 Canteen and Skydeck — floor 15

**Ceilings.** None, anywhere in the tower: like the real building ("OB"), every
floor shows its bare concrete soffit with steel beams, ducts, pipes and cable trays
hung under it.

**Layout.** 4800 × 4000 cm, split by a glass screen. South of it, under a
roof, the canteen, laid out from the real building's staff canteen: the till
and coffee bar on the back (west) wall; to its right the hot-food line, with the
kitchen behind it (wet floor, one staff door); in front of those, three
timber-clad servery islands; then the dining hall, eighteen six-seat tables
widening out east towards the Skydeck gate. North of the screen, open to the
sky, the Skydeck balcony.

**Fights here.** The canteen is the densest cover in the game: eighteen tables
mean eighteen fire positions, the servery islands and the hot-food counter are
waist-high hard cover, and the kitchen is the one place to break line of sight. The screen is ordinary glass, so the canteen and the balcony can
see each other, and the one door in it - the Skydeck gate - is where the last
push happens. Squads arrive with one down, no utility and less than a magazine
each, and the design intent is that it *feels* like that.

**The balcony.** The roof core stands in the middle of it. On the east wing, the
extraction pad (900 cm hold radius) marked in safety yellow; on the west, the bar
pavilion and the winch control room; along the north edge, a glass balustrade. The winch rig, three air-handling units, four floodlight masts, a steel
balustrade, planters, a winch control room.

**Fights here.** The ending, and the only place the game deliberately holds a
player still. The pad is exposed on three sides with cover on one: the AHU boxes.
The hold is 30 seconds. Contested freezes the timer, so the fight is over the pad
itself, not the clock.

**Lighting.** Dusk, floodlit pools, hard shadows. The deck is the most beautiful
frame in the game and it is the one that goes on the store page.

**Extraction.** `Broadcast` 10 s → `Arriving` 45 s → `Holding` (30 s, frozen when
contested) → `Complete`. A failed run ends on this deck, in view of the
helicopter, which is the correct place for it.

**Interactables.** Two actors, and the mode is unplayable without them. The winch
console (`AOneBrahamExtractionTerminal`) is bolted to the pad-facing wall of the
winch control room, facing the deck, and it is the only caller of
`ServerCallExtraction` in the project: hold `E` for 1.4 s
(`AOneBrahamExtractionTerminal::CallHoldSeconds`, the length of
`AM_OB_Interact_Radio`) and the winch is called. Stand with your back to the pad
to use it, which is the decision the whole mode is built around. The winch pad
(`AOneBrahamExtractionPad`) is the 900 cm hold volume
(`C.EXTRACTION_HOLD_RADIUS_CM`, the same number as
`UOneBrahamMatchSettingsAsset::ExtractionZoneRadiusCm`): it registers the wire's
position with the extraction component at BeginPlay, and its floodlight is the
only light on the deck that moves — dark on standby, sweeping while the
helicopter is inbound, hard on the pad while a squad holds it, red while it is
contested. The console's screen mesh wants `M_OB_Screen` from the art pass
(`Docs/10` section 2): its `ScreenTint` parameter is what the console writes, and
with any other material the four states are told through the screen light alone.

### 2.8b The crown — floors 16 and 17

The real building has eighteen storeys and steps back at 15 and 17. Above the
canteen and the Skydeck the crown stands over the canteen half and the core
stalk (`CROWN_STALK_X`, `CROWN_NORTH_Y`); the rest of the plate stays the open
balcony.

* **16 - Management Suite.** A walled suite, a lounge of low sofas and timber
  tables, and the foot of the **atrium**: planters and multi-stem trees under
  a void (`ATRIUM_VOID`) that opens through 17 to the glass roof.
* **17 - Winter Garden and roof terrace.** A glass-roofed bar on steel ribs
  round the atrium void (glass balustrades), and through a glass screen the
  open **roof terrace**: pale paving, charcoal planters of grasses and trees,
  a balustrade at the drop to the Skydeck.

The lockdown closes 15 *and everything above it* to the lifts: a car bound for
17 stops below the Skydeck, or it could drop a squad a flight above the deck.

### 2.8c The sky

A photographed CC0 sky (`kloofendal_48d_partly_cloudy_puresky`, Poly Haven) on
a 10 km dome with the `M_OB_SkyDome` material: an IsSky unlit sample of the
cubemap, the photograph's sun clamped (the real sun is the directional light,
aimed at the sun in the photograph from the brightest pixel `fetch_open_assets`
finds), and the bottom 12.5 degrees blended into a London haze the height fog
matches. The skylight captures the dome, so the ambient matches the sky you see.
A dusk preset (`belfast_sunset_puresky`) is built in for the finale.

### 2.9 Outside — Aldgate East, from the map

The tower stands in the real neighbourhood. `build_district` extrudes
`Tools/Python/onebraham/data/aldgate_district.json`, which `district_data.py`
makes from OpenStreetMap extracts:

* **Every building within 600 m** - Leman Street, Alie Street, the
  Whitechapel High Street frontage, Goodman's Fields, Aldgate Place - from its
  real footprint, to its mapped height or its storey count x 3.5 m + roof.
* **The surrounding city to 1.8 km**, simplified, so the towers on the skyline
  stand in streets.
* **The City cluster** - 30 St Mary Axe, 22 Bishopsgate, Heron Tower, Lloyd's -
  and the other towers over 70 m within 2.6 km.
* **The streets**: carriageways at their class width, granite kerbs, centre
  dashes and London's double yellow lines; standing traffic on the main roads
  (red double-deckers, black cabs); lamp columns every 25 m.
* **Braham Street Park** and every green space, and the street trees.

**The frame.** The real One Braham (OSM way 100299970, 1 Braham Street) fixes
it: origin at its footprint's centroid, X along its long axis, -Y out of its
north-west face onto Braham Street - where the game puts the revolving doors.
Geographic east/north go in as X = east, Y = south (Unreal is left-handed) and
are turned by the footprint's bearing (about 30 degrees), so the map is the right
way round rather than mirrored. Real scale, 1:1: the game tower's 44 x 36 m plate
sits inside the real building's footprint and the rest is forecourt.

**Facades** are four instances of `M_OB_Facade` - glass curtain wall, Portland
stone, London stock brick, dark rainscreen - which draws storey bands and window
bays from world position and the face normal, so any footprint gets windows
with no UVs, and lights a share of them from inside. Geometry is merged per
material by Geometry Script into a dozen `SM_District_*` meshes: fourteen
hundred buildings cost a dozen draw calls.

**The playable street** is the forecourt and Braham Street: blocking volumes
65 m out from the tower stop a sprint up Whitechapel High Street. The rest of
Aldgate is the view.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database Licence (ODbL
1.0). Rebuild the dataset with `python3 -I Tools/Python/onebraham/district_data.py
local.json sky.json tiles...` (the Overpass queries are in its docstring).

## 4. Built-in safety and fairness

Built into the generator, not bolted on afterwards:

| Rule | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum distance between opposing spawn anchors, **same storey** | 2,000 cm | no spawn-kills in the first five seconds |
| Minimum distance between same-cluster anchors | 600 cm | a squad spawns together but not inside each other |
| Vertical distance exempts the enemy rule | 400 cm (one storey) | a slab is a real barrier; the stairwells are the only route between floors |
| Spawn re-validation | cluster distances re-checked at place time, per storey | the generator reports every offending pair by name and floor |
| Every room has two exits | assert in the room programme | no room in this building is a death trap |
| No spawn in a room with a single exit | generator rule | a player must never be spawned into a corner |
| Navmesh rebuild on door and shutter state change | `AddDirtyArea` on the changed bounds | the AI and the geometry cannot disagree |
| 26 spawn anchors, 3 per area (4 in the two biggest) | `ANCHORS` in `build_environment.py` | every area can host every squad, so no squad is ever forced into the same room as another |
| Skydeck is never sealed by the lockdown | `bAllowLockdownClose = false` on that shutter | the building can trap you, it can never trap you away from the only way out |

## 5. What the map is responsible for

The map is not scenery. Three systems read the building itself, and each of them
reads a different property of the geometry the generator places. This section is
the contract between the level and those systems, and two of the three are
enforced by `validate_project.py`.

### 5.1 Floor finishes: fifteen surfaces, not one

Every floor is built as a slab in its area's material, and then every room gets a
finish laid on top of it — 4 mm proud, inset by the partition thickness, labelled
`F<floor>_<Room>_Finish`.

The finish is what the player's footsteps resolve from, what the bullet impact
family resolves from, and what the impact decal is chosen from. Without it the
whole tower is carpet, which sounds wrong, reacts wrong and is the single
cheapest way to make a game built inside one building feel like one room.

| Surface | Where it is, in the building |
| --- | --- |
| Marble | lobby slab, reception island, turnstile banks, lift lobby |
| Carpet | desk farms, meeting rooms, corner offices, waiting seating, breakout |
| Concrete | plaza slab, loading bay, under-stair stores, core, stairwell landings, every bare soffit |
| Metal | lift doors, handrails, riser cupboards, the exposed beams, ducts, pipes and cable trays under every soffit, the coffee bar's back wall |
| Glass / BallisticGlass | the perimeter glazing bands on floors 2–15 |
| Wood | the boardroom, the Skydeck bar, the canteen's servery islands, hot-food counter, till and tables |
| Puddle | kitchen floor, bike store — deliberately wet, deliberately loud |
| Grating | plant rooms, power rooms, comms cupboards, the Skydeck plant deck |
| Fabric | the acoustic meeting pods on the mezzanine and in open plan |
| ServerRack | the raised floor of the cold aisle, both server rows and the NOC |
| Plasterboard | every partition, the kitchen wall, all four breachable walls |
| Lino | canteen hall, servery, hot-food line, coffee bar, break-out kitchen, print alcove, security desk |
| Decking | the Skydeck balcony, the helipad, the edge rail |
| Flesh | gameplay only — a character hit resolves to it; no geometry uses it |

Two of these have gameplay attached, which is why the table matters:
**ServerRack** and **Grating** are the surfaces that throw sparks when shot, so
the server floors have to be rack floor rather than carpet for the sparks to
happen at all, and **Puddle** is the only surface that produces water spray.

The generator records where every surface ended up in the build manifest, and the
validator fails if an authored surface is used by nothing.

### 5.2 Effects the map triggers

The building is what makes the effects happen: a partition gives dust, a rack
gives sparks, a fall gives a body landing. `Docs/11` carries the full
seventeen-effect table with its call sites; the map's half of it is:

| The map provides | The effect that follows |
| --- | --- |
| Four breachable plasterboard partitions | `BreachExplosion`, `BreachDebris`, `DustPuff`, wall collapse audio |
| Rack floor and grating | `SparkShower` on every hit |
| Kitchen and bike-store floors | `WaterSpray` |
| Ballistic glazing on every floor edge | `GlassImpact`, glass decals, shatter audio |
| Fire shutters on floors 0–13 | `DustPuff` on the closing edge, 2.75 s of mechanism audio |
| Stairwell drops of one to three storeys | `CorpseImpact` when the body lands |
| The Skydeck pad in the open | smoke, flashes and the extraction fight happening in view of the helicopter |

### 5.3 Lighting, grade and audio, per floor

Each streaming floor level is created by `build_floor_level`, which places four
things besides the geometry, so a new floor gets all of them without anyone
remembering to add them — which is the point of generating the map rather than
placing it.

**Ceiling light grid.** `RectLight` panels on a 600 cm grid across the whole
floor plate, facing the floor, 4000 K, 16 000 lm, 1600 cm attenuation, movable
(the project disallows static lighting, so a static light is never baked and
Lumen gives it no bounce), fading out beyond 40 m. One fitting per ~36 m² puts
200–300 lux on the desks - a real office's level. Under each fitting, a
shadowless, diffuse-only **bounce light** (2400 lm) stands in for the light the
floor throws back onto walls; without it every wall that did not face a fitting
rendered pitch black. Each stair gets two bulkheads of its own, lobby and mid
landing, because the grid never lands inside the core.

**One `PostProcessVolume` per floor, bounded to that floor's slab** (plus 6 m
past the facade; floor 0 reaches out over the street, floor 15 up over the open
balcony). All floors are loaded at once, so eighteen unbound volumes would fight
over the whole building. Exposure is histogram auto-exposure in **EV100** (the
project sets ExtendDefaultLuminanceRange): 5.5–9.5 indoors, 6–12 on floor 15
so the eye adapts between the canteen and the balcony, biased per area from
`AREA_GRADE`'s first column. Bloom, film grain and vignette come from the
other columns. Every value is written with its `bOverride_` flag; without
those the volume silently keeps the engine default, which is how the grade
first failed to apply. A global unbound volume in the persistent level sits
underneath at priority -1 and sets no exposure at all.

| Area | Exposure bias source (min, max) | Bloom | Grain | Vignette |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground Plaza | 0.35 – 2.4 | 0.85 | 0.030 | 0.42 |

**No reflection captures, deliberately.** The project runs Lumen, which builds
its own reflection scene from the same geometry and lights. A per-floor capture
actor would be a capture that is never sampled and a second source of truth for
reflections — the kind of thing that produces a bug where one room reflects a
layout that was deleted three months ago.

**Every floor is attached to the persistent level, always loaded.** The
builder's last environment step adds each `L_OneBraham_Fxx` to
`L_Persistent_OneBraham` as a `LevelStreamingAlwaysLoaded` sub-level. (Before
that step existed the persistent map - the one the game opens - had the
gameplay actors and the facade but none of the floors.)

**One `AudioVolume` per floor**, scaled to the plate, enabled at priority 1. The
volume is the map telling the audio director where the room boundaries are; the
ambience bed itself is chosen from the zone id in the audio bank (`AMB_*`), so
the level and the mix cannot disagree about where the server room starts.

**One `NavMeshBoundsVolume` per floor**, sized to the plate. Combined with the
`AddDirtyArea` calls the doors and shutters make when they change state, this is
what stops the AI believing in a wall that was breached ten seconds ago.

## 6. Rebuilding and changing the map

```
Scripts\BuildContent.bat --only environment     rebuild the map
Scripts\BuildContent.bat --only environment,props --dress   and dress it
```

The builder writes `Tools/Python/onebraham_build_manifest.json`: every area,
floor, room, spawn anchor, access zone, terminal, breach wall, shutter and
surface, with locations. That manifest is what `validate_project.py` checks the
level against, which is how a level edit by hand gets caught before it becomes a
mystery bug three weeks later.

Adding a floor means adding an entry to `AREAS` and `ROOM_PROGRAMMES` in
`build_environment.py`. Adding a route up means adding an entry to `ACCESS_ZONES`.
Nothing in the map is hand-placed that can be generated, and everything that is
generated has a name you can search for in the outliner.
